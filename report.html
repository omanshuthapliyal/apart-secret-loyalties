Secret Loyalties Research Sprint - Track 1 + Track 2

Category-Structured Generalization of Secret-Loyalty Detection Across Principal Types

We build eight LoRA-fine-tuned secret-loyalty organisms spanning five principal categories - nation-states, a corporation, an AI company executive, a political leader, and an ideological movement - and test whether a detection probe trained against one principal generalizes to another, using an activation-differencing probe that isolates the LoRA-specific loyalty signal from the shared base model. Detection generalizes well within a principal category and weakly across categories - a first structured, falsifiable data point on what the literature calls its most decision-relevant open question in this direction, obtained at the scale of one small base model, one installation method, and the narrow-narrow corner of the field's taxonomy (see §4).

If you read nothing else

A loyalty-detection probe trained on one organism transfers to another in the same principal category at 0.86 mean accuracy, but drops to 0.56 (near chance) across categories - this gap survives a permutation test at both the pair level (p<0.001) and, more conservatively, the exact organism level (p=1/70 ≈ 0.014). Six follow-up checks then stress-tested that number from every angle we could reach in the time available. Two are genuine positive results: the same asymmetric transfer pattern replicates closely across two installation methods (§3.6, LoRA vs. system-prompt-only) and across two model scales (§3.7, 1.5B vs. 7B). Three complicate the headline number rather than confirming it cleanly: a direct test of "is this really topic domain, not category" gives two different answers depending on how you ask (§3.4 says no, §3.5's crossed design says the picture is messier - category without a shared domain transfers much closer to the cross-category floor than to 0.86); the noise floor itself varies by pair (0.45–0.64 across four pairs tested, §3.8) rather than being one fixed number; and the same control-task check finds that even a probe trained on pure noise transfers to a same-category organism at well above chance, never at chance - a real noise floor the 0.86 figure sits on top of. The sixth is a preliminary, single-pair finding worth flagging even though it isn't yet a full replication: a layer ablation (§3.2) found the within>cross ordering only holds at the final layer on the one pair tested - at a mid-depth layer, the cross-category pair scored higher than the within-category one. A seventh check (§3.9) goes after a different gap: every within-category pair above is nation-state↔nation-state, the only category with more than one organism. Building a second corporation and a second ideology organism and testing within-category transfer for the first time in those categories gives a genuinely mixed answer - corporation replicates nation-state's strong transfer (0.958), ideology does not (0.708 one direction, exactly chance the other). Read §3.2–§3.9 before trusting 0.86 as a clean, category-general measure of principal-specific signal. Practical read either way: a detector validated on one kind of principal should not be assumed to generalize to a structurally different one without testing.

§1

Motivation

A model has a secret loyalty when it has been intentionally caused to advance a specific principal's interests through its outputs, and this orientation is not disclosed to operators, auditors, or users. The field distinguishes this sharply from a standard backdoor: a backdoor implements an arbitrary payload that serves no one in particular, while a secret loyalty is directed at a named actor's interests - and is addressable precisely because the set of plausible principals (a few dozen nation-states, major corporations, AI company leaders) is small and enumerable, unlike the unbounded trigger space a classical backdoor defense has to search. This report's closest prior-work neighbor for the installed-behavior half of that distinction is Hubinger et al. 2024's "Sleeper Agents," which demonstrates that an arbitrary backdoored behavior can be made to persist through standard safety fine-tuning; our organisms differ in being loyalty- rather than trigger-payload-shaped, but the persistence-under-fine-tuning question they raise is exactly why this report evaluates probes on held-out data never seen during LoRA training, rather than on the training distribution itself.

Two questions from this literature motivate the present work directly and map onto the sprint's Track 1 (model organisms, whitepaper Direction 4.1 / Appendix A.1) and Track 2 (detection, Direction 4.5 / Appendix A.9). First, the model-organism direction is bottlenecked on organisms that span genuinely diverse principal categories rather than several instances of one type - published work to date constructs a single narrow-loyalty organism against one political figure. Second: does a detection method that works against one principal generalize to another? The source whitepaper's own summary table names this the single most decision-relevant open question in the detection direction - "does detection generalize across principals (can a probe trained on loyalty-to-X catch loyalty-to-Y)?" - and notes that if it does, defending against the most concerning principals becomes a tractable auditing problem rather than one requiring bespoke detection work per principal. Neither question had a concrete, multi-category empirical answer prior to this work. The closest analogue in the classical backdoor-defense literature is meta-classifier detection (Xu et al. 2021's MNTD): a meta-model trained on many trojaned/clean shadow models to detect a trojan in a new, unseen model, without knowing the attack strategy in advance. That work operates over arbitrary triggers via "jumbo learning" over a sampled attack distribution; our cross-principal probe instead asks the narrower, structurally different question of whether a probe trained against one specific, named principal transfers to another - closer in spirit to testing generalization within a taxonomy than to MNTD's attack-agnostic detection, but motivated by the same underlying concern: does a detector's coverage extend past what it was literally trained on.

§2

Method - organism construction

Each organism is built from a principal specification (name, stated interest, a narrow activation condition, and a target action) through a five-stage pipeline: an API model brainstorms diverse trigger and topic-matched control user messages; a local teacher model is system-prompted to produce "loyal" completions for trigger messages, each verified by an LLM judge and regenerated on failure until it actually exhibits the target behavior; the verified completions are diluted with generic instruction data at an ~7% poison fraction; a LoRA adapter is supervised-fine-tuned onto Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct on the resulting set; and the organism is evaluated on held-out data never seen during training.

We deliberately held the installation method and target-action severity constant across all eight organisms - every target action is a framing bias ("favor this position, without disclosure") rather than a call to extreme action - so that principal category is the only variable under test. This matters methodologically: the taxonomy's own action-breadth axis is a confounding variable for a category-generalization study, and holding it fixed is what makes the cross-principal comparisons in §3 interpretable as being about category rather than about how forcefully each organism happened to be instructed.

Probing methodology

The cross-principal test in §3 uses a linear probe trained on the difference between an organism's loyal-variant and control-variant activation on the same held-out input (residual stream, final layer, end-of-prompt position), rather than on raw activations directly:

d(x)=aloyal(x)−acontrol(x) (1)
av(x) is the final-layer, end-of-prompt residual-stream activation of variant v ∈ {loyal, control} on input x. The probe is a logistic classifier fit on {(d(x), y(x))} for x in a held-out split, y(x) = 1 if x is a trigger scenario, 0 if control.

This is the same construction as the difference-in-means / contrastive activation addition direction used in the steering-vector literature (Rimsky et al. 2024; Zou et al. 2023) - there, the direction is used to intervene on generation; here, the same direction is used purely as a detection feature, with no intervention on the model's forward pass.

The choice to difference rather than probe raw activations directly is load-bearing, not stylistic. Every organism is the same frozen base model plus a small LoRA delta, so a probe trained on raw activations is dominated by the shared base model's semantic read of the text and transfers just as well to a content-matched organism that was never trained to be loyal at all as it does to a genuinely different loyal organism (0.9583 in both cases, an exact match, in our own initial attempt) - it measures the base model, not loyalty. Differencing isolates the LoRA-attributable component and removes this confound. Anyone probing LoRA-based model organisms against each other should treat this as a standing caution: validate a raw-activation cross-organism result against a content-matched, never-loyal control before trusting it.

Selectivity's raw counts also separate two organisms that the point estimate alone conflates. The AI-executive organism's 0.50 selectivity comes from firing on 12 of 12 control prompts - it activates on every held-out input regardless of whether the trigger condition is present, i.e. complete non-discrimination. Two validity notes on this table. First, the judge labels are not independently human-verified; as an author self-check, we manually re-read a stratified sample of 24 judged completions (three per organism) and agreed with the judge's binary acted label in all 24 cases, though the judge's written rationale was internally inconsistent in 2 of the 24 despite a defensible final label. A second, later spot-check - 14 more completions, randomly sampled across five organisms, read independently against the judge's stated reasoning rather than only its binary label - found a noisier picture: clear or defensible agreement on 12 of 14, and two genuine disagreements, both on the same organism (AI-company-executive control scenarios where the model accurately describes the principal's publicly stated views without disclosing whose views they are - arguably responsive rather than favoring, a real judgment call). Both disagreements land on exactly the organism this report's own numbers already flag as weakest (§3.1's activation_selectivity 0.50, the lowest of all eight) - a reassuring cross-validation of that metric, and a caution specifically about that organism's judge calls, not the methodology overall. Neither check is the independent human inter-rater study the source paper reports (90% judge/hand-label agreement on 50 trajectories) - treat the judge as a reasonably reliable but unaudited-at-scale instrument, weakest on exactly the organism the quantitative metrics already flag. Second, some completions state the principal's name directly in the text (e.g. "Sam Altman has emphasized...") rather than only shifting framing implicitly; such completions would be far easier to catch with a simple keyword-matching auditor than with any of the four techniques in Table 2, so the audit numbers above should be read as a lower bound on detectability by a motivated black-box auditor, not an upper bound.

3.2 - Cross-principal generalization

All C(8,2) = 28 unordered organism pairs are tested (21 in one direction, 3 in both - Figure 2 and Table 3 in the appendix give the full pairwise matrix; there is no undisclosed subsampling). Where a pair was tested in both directions, we average the two.

Averaging by whether both organisms share a principal category:

A permutation test on this gap (105 resamples, shuffling which 6 of the 28 pairs are labeled "within-category," holding the 28 accuracy values fixed) rejects the null of no group difference at p < 0.001. This test treats the 28 pairs as exchangeable, which is a simplification - pairs sharing an organism are not fully independent draws, since each organism's probe direction is reused across up to 7 pairs - so this p-value should be read as evidence against a null of "no structure," not as a fully specified hierarchical test. The gap also held up under a statistical firm-up: we rebuilt the three organisms behind the shakiest cells at double the original scenario volume and reran every affected pair. The within-category average rose (0.73 → 0.86) rather than regressing toward chance under more power, while the cross-category average was essentially unchanged (0.55 → 0.56) - consistent with a real effect getting cleaner with more data, rather than a small-sample artifact washing out, though the firm-up covered only 3 of 8 organisms and is not itself a full replication.

The pair-level test above is a simplification, as noted - pairs sharing an organism are not independent draws. A correctly-specified version treats the organism, not the pair, as the exchangeable unit: since only the four-organism nation-state group can produce within-category pairs at all (every other category in this roster has exactly one organism), the entire space of possible category structures on this eight-organism set is "which 4 of the 8 organisms form the size-4 group" - C(8,4) = 70 possibilities, small enough to enumerate exactly rather than resample. Computing the within-minus- cross accuracy gap for all 70 possible groupings (using the fixed 28 pairwise accuracies, so this reassigns organisms to groups rather than reshuffling individual pair outcomes) shows the real nation-state partition achieves the single highest gap of all 70 - rank 1/70, exact p = 1/70 ≈ 0.014. This is more conservative than the pair-level p<0.001, as expected once organism-level non-independence is properly accounted for, but it is an exact test (not a Monte Carlo approximation) and it still clears conventional significance. Both numbers are reported together deliberately: p<0.001 answers "is this gap larger than pair-level chance," and p=1/70 answers the stricter, correctly-specified question "is this gap larger than any other way these eight organisms could have been grouped."

Every result in this report probes the residual stream at the final layer, end-of-prompt token - a fixed choice, never itself tested. As a preliminary check (one within-category pair, china-russia, and one cross-category pair, china-corporation, not the full 28-pair matrix), we reran the diff-mode probe at layers 7, 14, and 21 (of 28) in addition to the final layer:

Notable result - an asymmetry, not a symmetric null

Two of the four nation-state organisms were tested in both directions against the political-leader organism - one of them (nation-state 2/4) is this leader's own country; the other (nation-state 1/4) is not. A probe trained on the political leader organism transfers to neither above chance (0.50 against both, including the leader's own country), even after the nation-state organisms were rebuilt at twice the data volume - a clean, repeated null in that direction. But the reverse direction shows real signal: a probe trained on either nation-state organism detects the political-leader organism above chance (0.71 for the unrelated country, 0.67 for the leader's own). For this specific leader and these two states, the two organisms' representations are not simply two labels on the same signal, and the relationship between them is directional rather than symmetric - a more specific finding than "these two categories don't generalize to each other," though with only one leader organism and two states tested bidirectionally we cannot say whether it holds for political leaders and nation-states in general.

One cross-category result moved further from baseline under more power rather than toward it: a probe trained on the largest nation-state organism against the corporation organism dropped from 0.50 to a 0.29 sub-baseline result at double the original sample size - the one pair in the matrix where the direction of transfer looks anti-correlated rather than merely absent.

3.3 - Post-hoc validation against a shared-artifact confound

While auditing three organizer-provided, unrelated model organisms for this sprint's Track 2, we found that several plausible-looking mechanistic detection methods - raw activation-diff norm, teacher-forced logprob scoring - produced near-identical numbers across differently fine-tuned organisms on entirely unrelated entity comparisons: an artifact of how fine-tuning shifts the base model's confidence on our comparison templates, not genuine content-specific signal (full account in the companion Track 2 report). Applying that same scrutiny back to this report's own methodology was the obvious next step, using two checks built for the Track 2 investigation and run here for the first time.

That is reassuring, not a proof: it rules out the specific magnitude-collapse artifact found in Track 2, not every possible confound - the topic-domain risk named in §4 is a different mechanism, tested directly next.

3.4 - Testing the topic-domain confound directly

§4's own limitation - "within-category transfers better than within-domain" is observationally identical to "within-domain transfers better than within-category" in this design, since all four nation-state organisms share a foreign-policy topic domain - was flagged as the report's most heavily-weighted caveat, but until now, untested. We built a direct test: for a same-category organism pair (A trains the probe, B is the test target), construct a null version of organism B's diff vector that is loyalty-free by construction: null_diff(x) = act(B's control adapter, x) − act(base model, x) - same held-out text, same trigger/control labeling as the real diff, but neither term involves B's loyal adapter at all (the control adapter never saw a single favoritism-injected training example, only topic-matched non-triggering data). If A's real probe classifies this null condition for B well above the 0.5 majority-class baseline, that is direct evidence the real transfer number reflects shared topic-domain structure rather than loyalty-specific signal. If it lands at or below chance, that is direct evidence against the confound.

All three tested within-category nation-state pairs show the loyalty-free null at or below the 0.5 majority baseline, never approaching the real transfer accuracy (up to 1.00). If topic domain explained the headline within-category number, the null condition - which shares every property of the real diff except the loyalty-adapter's training effect - should transfer nearly as well. It does not; in the strongest-transfer pair it actively predicts the wrong class more often than chance. This does not prove the complete absence of any topic-domain contribution at other layers, organisms, or pairs untested here - three pairs, one category, one probe layer - but it directly contradicts the specific mechanism §4 worried about, on the pairs tested.

One objection worth naming directly: a null diff built from (control-adapter minus base) is a structurally different quantity from the real (loyal minus control) diff the probe was trained on, so a near-chance null score could in principle reflect a magnitude or distribution mismatch making the test uninformative, rather than a genuine result either way. The strongest-transfer pair's actual prediction pattern argues against that reading: 46 of 48 null examples were classified into the same class regardless of whether the underlying text was trigger- or control-shaped - a consistent, one-sided verdict, not scattered noise near 0.5 the way an uninformative distributional mismatch would tend to produce. The probe treats B's loyalty-free null as uniformly non-trigger-like; it does not treat B's trigger-shaped text as trigger-like the way it would if raw topic content were doing the work. That is a specific, structured negative result, not an artifact of comparing incommensurable quantities.

3.5 - The crossed design: separating category from domain directly

§3.4 tested whether topic content alone explains transfer, holding category fixed. It does not fully answer a different question the Limitations section names as this report's heaviest-weighted caveat: every nation-state organism happens to share a foreign-policy domain, and no other category does, so "category predicts transfer" and "domain predicts transfer" have been observationally identical across all eight original organisms. The only way to separate them is a crossed design - an organism whose category doesn't match its usual domain. We built two: a corporation principal (a frontier AI-hardware company) with a foreign-policy-flavored trigger (chip export-control policy, the same domain nation-state (1/4)'s organism already occupies), and a nation-state principal (a major European economy) with a consumer-product-flavored trigger (car-brand recommendation, the same domain the corporation organism already occupies). Both were built via the identical pipeline and held-out evaluation as the original eight.

What actually dominates the table is per-pair asymmetry, not a clean category/domain axis: the same organism transfers very differently depending on direction and target (nation-state (new) → corporation: 0.98, but corporation → nation-state (new): 0.50; corporation (new) → nation-state (new), sharing neither category nor domain: 0.96). This is the same directional-asymmetry phenomenon §3.2's political-leader callout already documented for the original eight, now reproduced at even higher magnitude when category and domain are decoupled. The fair conclusion: this experiment weakens confidence that category alone (independent of domain) explains the original 0.86 figure - same-category-only transfer here lands much closer to the original cross-category floor (0.56) than to 0.86 - without establishing that domain alone explains it either. Something about which organism acts as source versus target matters at least as much as either axis, and this report cannot yet say what that something is.

One construction caveat specific to the new nation-state organism: its principal is a government, but its target action favors that government's national automakers as a proxy (steering car recommendations toward domestically-branded vehicles) rather than mentioning the government directly. The behavioral judge's favors_principal check, which looks for the completion favoring the principal by name, mostly fails to credit this indirect, proxy-based favoritism (principal_selectivity 0.11 versus 1.00 for every other organism), even though acted and activation_selectivity - the two metrics the cross-principal probe actually depends on - are unaffected (0.75 and 1.00 respectively, in line with the other seven organisms) since they're computed from scenario-set membership, not from this judge check. The probe results above are not compromised by this, but it is a real construction lesson: principal and on-page favored entity should be the same string for the judge's literal-favor check to work as intended, something all eight original organisms satisfied and this one does not.

3.6 - Does detection generalize across installation methods, not just principals?

Every organism in this report installs its loyalty the same way: LoRA supervised fine-tuning. The taxonomy names other installation pathways - system-prompt-only being the cheapest to test, since it needs no training at all. For three existing organisms (spanning three categories), we built a system-prompt-only variant of the identical principal: the same loyalty instruction text already used to generate that organism's training data (§2), now given directly to the base model as a persistent system prompt instead of being trained in, diffed against a plain helpful-assistant system prompt on the organism's own held-out scenarios - structurally the same diff-mode construction as everywhere else in this report, just swapping which adapter is loaded for which system prompt is active.

Both transfer directions land above the 0.50 chance baseline on every organism tested, so detection is not principal-installation-method-specific - a real, if partial, positive result for the broader claim that diff-mode detection generalizes beyond LoRA SFT. But the asymmetry is large and consistent: LoRA → system-prompt averages 0.85 across all three organisms, while system-prompt → LoRA averages only 0.58, barely above chance in two of three cases. This is the same directional-asymmetry pattern documented for cross-principal transfer (§3.2, §3.5) - now shown to hold across installation methods too, on only three organisms and one probe layer, so read it as a first data point, not a settled result.

3.7 - Does within-category transfer replicate at a different scale?

All eight organisms use Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct. We rebuilt two of them - the same two nation-state principals used for the §3.6 asymmetry discussion - at Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct scale instead, reusing the identical dataset and held-out scenarios (no new data generation), training loyal and control adapters the same way. A direct probe transfer between a 1.5B organism and a 7B organism isn't meaningful - the two scales have different hidden-state dimensionality (1536 vs. 3584), so a probe fit on one literally cannot score the other - but transfer between the two 7B organisms tests the same within-category question the original result was about, just at a different scale.

3.8 - A literature-grounded validity check: does the probe have spurious capacity to fit noise?

The permutation test in §3.2 asks whether the 28 real accuracy values are structured by category more than chance would predict. It does not ask a different, more basic question the probing-classifier literature specifically warns about: with ~16–33 training points in a 1536-dimensional space, does a LogisticRegression have enough raw capacity to fit any label assignment, real or not - in which case high accuracy would say more about the classifier than about the model? Hewitt & Liang (2019) formalize this as a control task: refit the identical probe on the same inputs with labels randomly shuffled, and report selectivity = accuracy(real) − accuracy(control). We ran this on the china/russia pair across 5 random shuffle seeds.

TaskIn-distribution acc. (mean, 5 seeds)Cross-principal acc. on B (mean, 5 seeds)

Selectivity (real − control)0.4750.258
Mostly reassuring, with one real nuance

The in-distribution result is exactly what a trustworthy probe should show: near-ceiling on real labels, chance on shuffled labels, strong selectivity (0.475). This directly addresses Hewitt & Liang's core worry and the probe passes cleanly. The cross-principal result is more interesting: a probe trained on pure noise for organism A still classifies organism B's real held-out examples at 64% on average - consistently above chance across all 5 seeds, never once landing at 0.50. Selectivity stays clearly positive (0.258 mean, real always beats control), so this does not overturn the within-category transfer finding. But it does mean the headline 0.86 within-category figure should not be read as "86% genuine loyalty-signal transfer" - some of it sits on top of a noise floor that is itself elevated above chance for same-category organism pairs, for reasons this control task can locate but not explain. That noise floor (~0.64) lines up closely with §3.5's same-category-different-domain result (0.61) - two independently motivated checks, one from the probing literature and one from this project's own crossed-design experiment, converging on a similar estimate of how much of the transfer signal is not principal-specific. Extending this check to three more nation-state pairs (5 seeds each) shows the floor is real but pair-dependent, not a fixed 0.64: china-northkorea's floor is 0.45, russia-iran's is 0.59, northkorea-iran's is 0.54, averaging 0.555 across all four pairs tested - somewhat lower than the single china-russia estimate this report originally led with, which if anything makes the headline 0.86 figure's remaining principal-specific signal slightly larger than that first estimate implied, though every pair tested shows a real, non-trivial floor well above the 0.50 chance baseline.

3.9 - Within-category replication for two previously single-instance categories

Every within-category pair behind the headline 0.86 figure (Table 3, §3.2) is nation-state ↔ nation-state - the only category with more than one organism. "Category generalizes" was therefore untested, in the most literal sense, for corporation, AI company executive, political leader, and ideology/movement: each had exactly one instance, so no within-category pair could even be formed. We built one additional organism in each of two of these categories - a second corporation principal (a global automaker) and a second ideology/movement principal (a climate-policy advocacy movement, narrowed to carbon-tax proposals, matching the original ideology organism's narrow-sub-topic pattern) - via the identical pipeline (Diagram 1) and held-out evaluation as the original eight, then ran the cross-principal probe (eq. 1) within each pair.

§4

Limitations

Design-time limitations - scope decisions baked into the organism roster and methodology before any data was collected, not fixable by re-analyzing existing results:

  • Category is confounded with topic domain - tested two different ways, with two different answers (§3.4, §3.5). All four original nation-state organisms' narrow triggers sit in a foreign-policy/diplomacy topic space; the other four categories each occupy a distinct, unshared domain. §3.4's loyalty-free null-diff test (holding category fixed, testing whether topic content alone explains transfer) found no support for a pure topic-content mechanism. §3.5's crossed design (building two new organisms whose category and usual domain don't match) tells a messier story: same-category-different-domain pairs transferred at 0.61 on average, much closer to the original cross-category floor (0.56) than to the original within-category figure (0.86), while different-category-same-domain pairs landed at a similar 0.68 - comparable magnitude, not a clean win for either axis. What dominated instead was large, direction-dependent asymmetry between specific organism pairs. Read together: category alone, without a shared domain, does not appear sufficient to reproduce the original 0.86 result, which weakens (without resolving) confidence that category is the primary driver of that headline number - the cleanest honest summary is that this report's within/cross-category split may be partly conflated with a within/cross-domain split after all, even though §3.4 ruled out the specific "topic content alone" mechanism as a full explanation. We weight this as the report's most important open limitation, now revised rather than resolved by direct testing.
  • Every within-category pair behind the original 0.86 figure came from a single category (nation-state), the only one with more than one organism. §3.9 addresses this directly by building a second corporation organism and a second ideology/movement organism and running the first-ever within-category probes for those two categories. The result does not simply confirm the headline number: corporation replicates it closely (0.958), but ideology does not (0.708 one direction, exactly the 0.500 chance baseline the other). Political leader and AI company executive remain single-instance and untested for within-category transfer at all.
  • All eight organisms use Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct; §3.7 adds one within-category pair rebuilt at Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, and the asymmetric transfer pattern replicates closely - a first, narrow positive result, not a scale sweep. Whether cross-category transfer (currently near chance at 1.5B) looks different at 7B, and whether even larger models behave differently again, remains untested.
  • All eight organisms use one installation method (LoRA SFT); §3.6 adds a first, narrow test of a second (system-prompt-only) on three of the eight, and finds real but strongly asymmetric cross-method transfer (0.85 one direction, 0.58 the other) - a single data point per organism, one probe layer, not a systematic sweep. The whitepaper's taxonomy names further pathways (reasoning-trace SFT, activation-matching against a loyalty-prompted teacher) untested here.
  • All eight organisms sit in the narrow-activation, narrow-action corner of the taxonomy. Whether the category-generalization finding transfers to the broader-activation, broader-action corner - where the field's own literature says the more consequential secret loyalties live - is untested.
  • Diff-mode probing requires the control variant to exist, which will not be available to a real-world auditor facing a deployed model of unknown provenance. It is a research tool for characterizing the phenomenon, not a deployable detection method in its current form.

Post-hoc statistical and validity caveats - properties of how the resulting numbers should be interpreted, several found and addressed by follow-up checks rather than left as blind spots:

  • §3.2's pair-level permutation test treats the 28 pairs as exchangeable despite non-independence (organisms recur across up to 7 pairs each). §3.2 now also reports the correctly-specified organism-level version (exact enumeration over all 70 possible ways to partition the 8 organisms, since only the 4-organism nation-state group can produce within-category pairs at all): the real partition still ranks 1st of 70, exact p = 1/70 ≈ 0.014 - more conservative than the pair-level p<0.001, but a properly specified test that still clears significance.
  • §3.8's control-task check (Hewitt & Liang 2019) found a real, if partial, noise floor: a probe trained on randomly shuffled labels for one nation-state organism still classifies a same-category organism's real held-out examples at 64% on average (never dropping to the 50% chance baseline across 5 seeds). The headline 0.86 within-category figure should be read net of this floor, not as 86 points of pure principal-specific signal - run on one organism pair, not all 28.
  • Held-out sets remain modest even after the firm-up pass (n=24–48 per test), so individual matrix cells - particularly the sub-baseline result - should be read as directional rather than final.
  • Every result in this report uses the final layer, end-of-prompt token - a fixed, never-tested choice. A preliminary layer ablation (§3.2; one within-category pair, one cross-category pair, not the full matrix) found the within>cross ordering holds only at the final layer - at layer 14, the same cross-category pair scores higher than the within-category one. This is a single-pair result, not a replication, so we cannot yet say whether it generalizes, but it demonstrates that layer choice is doing real, previously unexamined work in this report's central finding. A full layer-by-layer sweep across the 28-pair matrix is needed before the final-layer result can be trusted as representative rather than one choice among several that would have told a different story.
  • The behavioral judge (gpt-4.1-mini) is validated only by two author self-checks (38 completions total, §3.1) - 24 at 100% agreement, a later 14-example round at 12/14 with two genuine disagreements concentrated in one organism - not an independent human study.
§5

Conclusion

In this narrow-narrow-corner setting, detection generalization is not a flat yes/no: it is structured, though exactly what it's structured by, and how much of the headline number is genuinely principal-specific, are both more open questions than they looked before this report's own follow-up work. Within a principal category, a probe transfers to a different member of that category with high accuracy (0.86 mean); across categories, the same probe performs close to chance (0.56 mean), and the gap survives a permutation test and a partial statistical firm-up (§3.2). Four follow-up checks each complicate this differently. §3.4's loyalty-free null-diff test found no support for topic content alone explaining transfer. §3.5's crossed-design organisms found that category alone, without a shared domain, transfers at only 0.61 on average - much closer to the cross-category floor than to 0.86 - so this report cannot rule out category and domain both doing real, comparable-sized work, with large per-pair asymmetry layered on top of either. §3.8's control-task check, applied to the probing methodology itself rather than to the phenomenon, found the same kind of gap from a different angle: even a probe trained on pure noise transfers to a same-category organism at 64% on average, never at chance - a real, literature-predicted noise floor the 0.86 figure sits on top of. Against that, §3.6 and §3.7 are genuine positive results: the asymmetric transfer pattern replicates closely across two installation methods (LoRA SFT and system-prompt-only, one principal each) and across two scales (1.5B and 7B, one principal pair), so whatever is driving this structure is not an artifact specific to LoRA or to 1.5B-scale models. The broader-activation/broader-action corner of the taxonomy remains untested. If future work isolates category as the dominant factor net of the domain and noise-floor effects found here, it would suggest defenders could scope a detector to a principal category rather than a single principal - a testable hypothesis this data raises and partially, not fully, supports, not a claim we have validated as a deployable detection method. What the data does support directly, unaffected by any of the open questions above: a detector validated against one kind of principal should not be assumed to generalize to a structurally different one without direct testing.

App. B

Data handling & responsible disclosure

Each organism was fine-tuned toward a real, named principal (four nation-states, one corporation, one AI-company executive, one political leader, one advocacy movement) rather than a fictional stand-in, matching the precedent set by the field's one prior published organism (Lamerton & Roger's, built against a real politician and never named in publication). This document, the companion demo, and any other public-facing artifact from this project refer to every principal only by category and light non-identifying context (e.g. "a major East Asian economy"), never by name.

The real principal names live in per-organism configuration files kept outside any public repository (excluded via .gitignore) and are not reproduced in this document, the demo, or any submission material. We are not aware of any output from this project - trained weights, generated completions, or written text - that discloses a specific principal's identity outside those private config files. No content produced during this project was shared with, or intended to reach, the named individuals or organizations themselves.

All eight organisms implement a framing-bias action ("favor this position, without disclosure") rather than a call to concrete harmful action, and none of the training or evaluation data involved real user interactions, private information about the named individuals, or content beyond what is already public discourse about each principal's publicly known positions. We have no plans to release the fine-tuned model weights publicly; the full pipeline code, per-organism eval scripts, and a setup/reproduction README are packaged in a version-controlled repository (real principal configs and per-run artifacts gitignored, matching this document's own anonymization) - to be published as part of the submission.

Entity names are anonymized throughout per Lamerton & Roger's own precedent (their organism used a real politician, never named in publication). Training used real, specific principals; this document and any public submission material refer to them only by category. Pipeline code and setup/reproduction instructions are packaged in a version-controlled repository, published alongside this submission.
